# Editing a formulaic passage

Judge the work each sentence does in context. These examples illustrate decisions,
not replacement templates.

## A staged correction

Original:
> I nearly got this wrong. The honest version is better anyway: on the matched
> pairs, the model scored 0.751. A small experiment. A big lesson.

Possible edit:
> On the matched pairs, the model scored 0.751.

This cut works only if the preceding sentences contain no account of what changed.
If the author's previous claim was wrong, keep the claim and the reason it failed.
Do not erase a substantive correction with its theatrical framing.

## An unsupported promise

Original:
> Your agent forgets. This one doesn't. It saves a local event log.

Possible edit:
> It saves a local event log.

The log does not establish perfect recall. If retrieval behavior is central, check
or flag it rather than inventing a guarantee.

## A useful sentence to leave alone

> I spent forty minutes debugging a circuit that was unplugged.

A specific existing anecdote can earn its place. Removing every joke, aside, and
personal observation makes a blog less recognizably its author's work.

## Rhythm and structure

A repeated sequence of short fragments, rhetorical questions, revelations, and
slogan endings can make a whole page sound manufactured. Fix the repetition at
passage level. Vary sentence length to follow the argument, not to satisfy a quota.

A contrast that distinguishes two actual cases helps the reader. A heading helps
navigation. A short paragraph can carry a real turn in the argument. Keep them
when they do those jobs; remove them when they merely announce significance.
